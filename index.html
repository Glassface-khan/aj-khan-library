The Door Held Open

Part III — The World
The bus was pulling out—he saw the light blink amber, doors beginning to slide to a close. An old man hurried through the thinning night, his cane behind him, tripping where the pavement rose. The driver could have gone; no one would blame a schedule kept, a stranger left behind. Instead he held the door, and made no claim to kindness—just stood there, patient, unresigned. I think of how much mercy moves this way— unsigned, unthanked, too brief to call a sign— until you notice how the whole world stays upright on hands like this, and not on mine.

About

Ali Jawwad Khan

Ali Jawwad Khan is a writer based in Ober-Ramstadt, Germany. His fiction is rooted in a long and serious engagement with Islamic history, theology, and the Sufi contemplative tradition — not as subject matter to be explained, but as a way of seeing that shapes how his stories are told. He is drawn to the questions that belong to no single faith: what a person owes the truth once they have profited from its absence; how mercy survives in people with no power to change anything except how gently they treat what remains. His approach trusts image over statement, and the ordinary over the grand, in the belief that the largest truths arrive most honestly through the smallest lives.
